# Narrow four claims, then build the connector

The project is sound and worth doing, and it is a good interview project, but two of its four novelty claims are overstated and need rewording before anyone who knows the area reads the proposal. The core mechanism holds up. RoPE re-rotation of cached keys is exact in real arithmetic for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3, and serving re-rotated chunks as a contiguous prefix fits vLLM 0.27.1's connector contract without touching engine source. As of October 2026 no merged vLLM feature and no working LMCache path does this ([LMCache #3238](https://github.com/LMCache/LMCache/issues/3238), [#4476](https://github.com/LMCache/LMCache/issues/4476), [#5101](https://github.com/LMCache/LMCache/issues/5101), [vLLM PR #32785](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/32785)). The "new setting for CacheBlend" claim is false, because AgentKVShift already ran CacheBlend on LoCoMo with Mistral-7B ([AgentKVShift](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21604)). The "like-for-like comparison" claim has to acknowledge an 11-method benchmark study that already compares EPIC and CacheBlend on Mistral-7B, though on RAG data rather than agent memory ([Cestola et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20218)). What remains novel is the combination: EPIC and naive re-positioning on conversational memory, an independent reimplementation of AgentKVShift (it has no released code), plugin-only serving in unmodified vLLM, and an end-to-end break-even sweep against prefix caching. Most of the engineering risk sits in details the papers leave out. The reference code differs from the papers (CacheBlend's code selects on V only, at one layer). Hugging Face's SDPA path silently changes the mask. vLLM's paged layout packs K and V into one tensor. The scheduler and worker halves of the connector share no Python state. For interviews the strongest asset is a predicted-versus-measured story. At 14 GFLOP of compute against 128 KiB of KV per token, loading cached KV should beat recomputing it by roughly 10-20× over PCIe and cost almost nothing from GPU memory. Explaining the gap between that ideal and the measured TTFT is the part interviewers will probe.

**How to read this report.** Statements with an inline link are verified against the cited source. Some of those sources were read only through search snippets, and where that matters the text says so. Sentences marked *(inference)* are reasoning or arithmetic done for this report, not something a source states. Numbers marked *est.* are back-of-envelope estimates. The project has produced no measurements yet, so every project result appears as a bracketed placeholder.

## Two of the four novelty claims survive unchanged

The proposal's framing is that the algorithms come from prior work and the contribution is engineering and evaluation. That framing is right, and it is the safest one, because the field moved fast in 2026. At least 30 non-prefix or position-independent KV-reuse methods now exist. They fall into four families: training-free selective recompute (CacheBlend, EPIC, ProphetKV, QCFuse, RelaxKV, CacheTune and others), training-free correction (AgentKVShift, KVCOMM, KVCMAS, APE), fine-tuned approaches (Block-Attention, TurboRAG, KVLink, SemPIC), and serving systems (LMCache, MiniPIC, Irminsul, Leyline, CacheSlide). Almost all of them were evaluated on RAG passages rather than conversation memory. Two recent selectors show where the strong-baseline bar now sits. ProphetKV keeps "96%-101% of full-prefill accuracy with only a 20% recomputation ratio" ([ProphetKV](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02579), snippet), and RelaxKV, released 27 September 2026, uses ProphetKV as its main competitor ([RelaxKV](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33503v1)). ProphetKV has replaced EPIC as the standard strong baseline: AgentKVShift, RelaxKV and QCFuse all compare against it.

The proposal quotes AgentKVShift correctly. Its LoCoMo table at a 10% recompute ratio gives these numbers for Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3 ([AgentKVShift](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21604)):

| Memory system | Full recompute | CacheBlend | ProphetKV | AgentKVShift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A-Mem | 0.305 | 0.208 | 0.170 | 0.282 |
| LiCoMemory | 0.509 | 0.290 | 0.357 | 0.491 |

Two details matter here. The LiCoMemory row the proposal cites is the favourable one. On A-Mem the full-recompute F1 is 0.305, and AgentKVShift's gap is 0.023, wider than the proposal's success criterion of 0.02 *(inference: plain conversation chunks could land anywhere in the 0.3-0.5 range, so relative gaps are the right comparison, as the proposal already says)*. And AgentKVShift has released no code, so method 4 will be a reimplementation from the paper, and the report should call it that.

Here is each claim from §3.3 checked against the literature.

## Getting C1 and C2 exactly right

### Chunks are token-ID lists, never re-tokenized text

The decided chunking (whole turns packed to about 256 tokens, each with a session-date header, tokenized once, assembled by concatenating token IDs) avoids the most common silent failure. Mistral v0.3 uses a SentencePiece `LlamaTokenizer` with `add_prefix_space: True` and `add_bos_token: True` ([tokenizer_config.json](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3/blob/main/tokenizer_config.json)). *(Inference:)* With SentencePiece, `encode(A) + encode(B)` can differ from `encode(A + B)` at the join, so the full-prefill reference must use exactly the concatenated IDs and never a re-tokenized string. Encode each chunk with `add_special_tokens=False`, start every chunk with the same fixed text (the `DATE: … CONVERSATION:` header that LoCoMo's own code uses, per [gpt_utils.py](https://github.com/snap-research/locomo/blob/main/task_eval/gpt_utils.py)), and assert `prompt_ids == concat(chunk_ids)` once per prompt. CacheBlend's example decodes its assembled prompt back to text and re-encodes it ([blend.py](https://github.com/YaoJiayi/CacheBlend/blob/main/example/blend.py)), which reintroduces exactly this risk. Don't copy that step.

The chat template is the second trap. Mistral v0.3 has no standalone system turn. A system message is held back and emitted inside the *last* `[INST]` as `system + "\n\n" + user` ([tokenizer_config.json](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3/blob/main/tokenizer_config.json)). *(Inference:)* Build the prompt by hand as `[BOS] [INST] system\n\n <chunk_1> … <chunk_k> \n\n <question + LoCoMo instruction> [/INST]` and check it once against `apply_chat_template`. Treat `[BOS][INST] system\n\n` as a fixed prefix that is never reused at a new position. Pick one convention for how each chunk is prefilled alone, record it, and apply it to every method. The recommended convention is `[BOS][INST] <chunk>`, keeping only the chunk's slice. A chunk prefilled with no BOS makes its own first token the attention sink, which is exactly EPIC's failure mode and would make method 1 look worse than it has to ([EPIC](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.15332)). The reference codebases disagree on this point: CacheBlend strips BOS from each chunk, while EPIC keeps every chunk's BOS ([EPIC main.py](https://github.com/DerekHJH/epic/blob/main/benchmarks_ours/evals/e2e/main.py)). That disagreement makes the convention a reasonable ablation.

### RoPE re-rotation: exact in math, position-dependent in floating point

RoPE (rotary position embedding) encodes position by rotating pairs of dimensions in each query and key by an angle proportional to the token's position. Mistral v0.3 uses unscaled RoPE with `rope_theta` = 1e6, head dimension 128, 32 layers, and 8 KV heads shared by 32 query heads (grouped-query attention, GQA). It has no `rope_scaling` and no sliding window ([config.json](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3/blob/main/config.json)). HF pairs dimension *i* with *i*+64 through `rotate_half` (the "NeoX" convention), not the adjacent pairs in the RoFormer paper. HF computes angles in fp32 but **casts cos and sin to the model dtype before multiplying**. Keys come out of a `DynamicCache` already rotated ([modeling_mistral.py](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/models/mistral/modeling_mistral.py)). 2-D rotations compose additively, so a key stored at position *p* moves to *p′* with one more rotation by Δ = *p′* − *p*. CacheBlend's appendix proves the attention score depends only on relative distance ([CacheBlend](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.16444)). Every token in a chunk shifts by the same Δ, so one cos/sin vector serves the whole chunk:

```python
inv_freq = model.model.rotary_emb.inv_freq.float()   # use the model's own, never recompute theta**(-2i/d)
ang = (new_start - canonical_start) * inv_freq        # Δ may be negative
cos, sin = torch.cat([ang, ang]).cos(), torch.cat([ang, ang]).sin()
k32 = k_stored.float()                                # [B, 8, T, 128], post-RoPE
k_new = (k32 * cos + rotate_half(k32) * sin).to(torch.bfloat16)   # V is never rotated
```

Three pitfalls follow *(inference)*. First, the interleaved RoFormer formula rotates the wrong pairs and fails silently, so use HF's own `rotate_half`. Second, always rotate from the stored canonical position, never chain old→mid→new, because each bf16 cast adds a rounding. Third, fp32 is not exact at large positions. The highest-frequency angle at position *p* is *p* radians, so its fp32 rounding error is about *p*·6e-8 (about 1e-3 rad at 16K). HF computes fl(*p*·f) while the delta path computes fl(*p*_old·f) + fl(Δ·f). A blanket 1e-6 tolerance will fail for correct code. Either build both reference and test from fp64 angles outside HF and use a tight tolerance, or scale the tolerance with position. For later extensions to other models: Llama-3-style scaling keeps delta rotation exact. YaRN multiplies cos and sin by an attention factor, so a delta built with that factor scales K twice. Dynamic NTK changes frequencies with sequence length, which delta rotation cannot fix ([modeling_rope_utils.py](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/modeling_rope_utils.py)).

### The per-layer forward: plain tensors, an explicit mask, and no FlashAttention-2

HF's mask builders assume the query tokens are a contiguous block ([masking_utils.py](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/masking_utils.py)). The SDPA integration has a trap: with `attention_mask=None` it sets `is_causal=True`, and when there are more keys than queries it **crops K and V to the first q_length tokens**, because SDPA aligns its causal mask upper-left ([sdpa_attention.py](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers/blob/main/src/transformers/integrations/sdpa_attention.py)). For a scattered recompute set that is silently wrong. *(Inference:)* Drive `model.model.layers[l]` yourself on plain `[1, 8, N, 128]` tensors rather than `DynamicCache`, whose `update` appends where you need scatter writes. Give each recomputed token its true absolute position and pass an explicit boolean mask `mask[q, kv] = pos[kv] <= pos[S][q]`. Write fresh K/V back **before** attention, so recomputed tokens see each other. Use `attn_implementation="sdpa"` or `"eager"`, because FlashAttention-2 cannot express an arbitrary mask. Run the full-prefill reference on the same backend, so kernel differences don't count as reuse error.

One structural constraint shapes all four methods *(inference)*. A token recomputed at layer *l* needs its fresh hidden state from layer *l*−1, which exists only if it was recomputed at every earlier layer. So the recompute set can only shrink from layer to layer (S_l ⊆ S_{l−1}). Methods that select at "layer 1" must therefore run layer 0 in full and compute layer-1 Q/K/V for every token, which is what CacheBlend's code does ([CacheBlend llama.py](https://github.com/YaoJiayi/CacheBlend/blob/main/vllm_blend/vllm/model_executor/models/llama.py)). The same reasoning gives E0's check (c): with S = all tokens at every layer and an exact mask, the loop computes the same function as `model(ids)`. Any larger mismatch is a bug in the mask, the positions, the write-back, or the final norm and LM head. For E0, turn TF32 off, enable `torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True)`, and set `CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG=:4096:8`.

### The four methods as the reference code actually implements them

The papers and their released code disagree in ways that change budget accounting, so build from the code, document every departure, and charge every method for its selection layer.

| Method | What the reference code does | Departures to note | Budget to report |
|---|---|---|---|

| 2. EPIC (LegoLink) | Recomputes the first *k* tokens of every retrieved chunk (the skipped "first chunk" is the system prompt, not the first document) at **every layer including 0**, plus the whole question. Recompute probes at every layer, estimate the per-layer chunk mean shift μ̂ from the probes, and add *w*·μ̂ to non-probes with *w* = min(d, 1), d unnormalized ([AgentKVShift](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21604)). *(Inference:)* Report a single budget, Σ_l |S_l| / (32 · N_reusable), with question tokens excluded from numerator and denominator and any full layer charged as |S_l| = N. Report a FLOPs ratio next to it. Linear layers cost about 0.44 GFLOP per token per layer (≈14 GFLOP per token across 32 layers), and selective attention costs about 4·s·N·4096 per layer. This is what makes "one budget accounting" real. No published method charges its own selection layer.

## Building C3 on vLLM 0.27.1 without editing vLLM

### What the connector contract allows

In v0.27.1 a KV connector subclasses `KVConnectorBase_V1` and is instantiated **twice**, once in the scheduler role and once in the worker role. The two copies share nothing except a `KVConnectorMetadata` object you define, which the scheduler builds each step and the runner binds before the forward pass ([base.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/distributed/kv_transfer/kv_connector/v1/base.py), [scheduler.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py), [mixin](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/v1/worker/kv_connector_model_runner_mixin.py)). The abstract methods are three on the scheduler side (`get_num_new_matched_tokens`, `update_state_after_alloc`, `build_connector_meta`) and four on the worker side (`start_load_kv`, `wait_for_layer_load`, `save_kv_layer`, `wait_for_save`). The class loads out of tree through `--kv-transfer-config '{"kv_connector":"ChunkReuseConnector","kv_connector_module_path":"pkg.module","kv_role":"kv_both","kv_connector_extra_config":{...}}'` ([factory.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/distributed/kv_transfer/kv_connector/factory.py), [kv_transfer.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/config/kv_transfer.py)). The key constraint is in the docstring: `get_num_new_matched_tokens` must report "the largest prefix of prompt-tokens for which KV cache is actually available", be side-effect free, and may be called several times. The contract is a contiguous prefix count. No gap or per-token bitmap method exists in 0.27.1. The PR that would add `get_computed_token_gaps()` has been open since January 2026 ([#32785](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/pull/32785)).

That constraint is why C3 works where LMCache's CacheBlend broke *(inference)*. LMCache keyed chunks by prefix-chained hashes, so a reused document behind a changed question got "Only 24/2451 tokens hit" ([#3238](https://github.com/LMCache/LMCache/issues/3238)). C3 hashes each chunk on its own and *places* the retrieved chunks as the prompt prefix, so non-prefix reuse happens inside the connector's lookup and re-rotation, not in vLLM's hit contract. The flip side is that one uncached token ends the hit. BOS, `[INST]`, the system text and every separator must also be in the store, as pseudo-chunks. Methods 2-4 need per-token recompute inside the prefix, which this contract cannot express, so keeping them in Transformers is the correct scope.

### Copy ExampleConnector, then fix four things

`ExampleConnector` (formerly SharedStorageConnector) is the template. It hashes prompt token IDs, builds `slot_mapping = block_id * block_size + offset` from `new_req.block_ids[0]` in `build_connector_meta`, and writes `dst[block_idxs, :, offsets] = src` per layer in `start_load_kv` by walking `forward_context.no_compile_layers` ([example_connector.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/distributed/kv_transfer/kv_connector/v1/example_connector.py)). Four things need changing *(inference unless cited)*.

The first is the lookup. `get_num_new_matched_tokens` must walk the prompt from `num_computed_tokens`, match each segment's token IDs against the store by exact hash (comparing tokens on a hash hit to rule out collisions), stop at the first uncovered token, and cache the parse per `request_id` so repeated calls stay side-effect free. Return `(n, False)`, a synchronous load. That avoids the `WAITING_FOR_REMOTE_KVS` state machine, and because `start_load_kv` runs outside the compiled model, it keeps the default CUDA-graph mode ([scheduler.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py), [base.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/distributed/kv_transfer/kv_connector/v1/base.py)).

The second is alignment. The scheduler and prefix cache work on whole 16-token blocks, and ExampleConnector notes that the scheduler expects block-aligned counts. Return `floor(L/16)*16 − num_computed_tokens`. vLLM then recomputes the remaining *L* mod 16 tokens of the last chunk with full attention, so E5's HF reference must apply the same truncation. ExampleConnector's own `align_to_block_size` returns `(n−1)//bs*bs`, which drops a whole block when *n* is an exact multiple of 16, so don't copy it. A hit is also capped at `num_tokens − 1` so at least one token produces logits ([kv_cache_manager.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/v1/core/kv_cache_manager.py)). Because the question always follows the chunks, the cap never bites.

The third is the write layout. FlashAttention and FlashInfer in v0.27.1 use a **packed** layout `(num_blocks, num_kv_heads, block_size, 2·head_size)`, with K in channels `[:128]` and V in `[128:]`, NHD physical order by default ([flash_attn.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/v1/attention/backends/flash_attn.py), [flashinfer.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/v1/attention/backends/flashinfer.py)). This differs from older releases. For Mistral each layer is `[num_blocks, 8, 16, 256]`, and a token at slot *s* is written as `kv[s//16, :, s%16] = cat([K_rot, V], -1)`. Assert the shape in `register_kv_caches` and fail loudly. vLLM applies neox-style RoPE before the cache write, like HF ([llama.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/model_executor/models/llama.py), [mistral.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/model_executor/models/mistral.py)), so the post-RoPE store with fp32 delta rotation carries over directly. Batch all layers into one tensor operation rather than many small per-chunk ops. Load the model in HF format rather than Mistral-native, so the q/k weight permutations match the Transformers stack for E5 (unverified whether 0.27.1 defaults to native format for this repo).

The fourth is process layout. The scheduler connector lives in the EngineCore process and the worker connector lives with the model runner. Design for no shared Python state. The scheduler loads a lightweight index (`token-hash → chunk_id, length`) from a path in `kv_connector_extra_config`, and the worker holds the tensors. A GPU-resident store competes with vLLM's KV block pool, so lower `--gpu-memory-utilization` to about 0.5-0.6, or keep the store in pinned host memory and pay the host-to-device copy.

One benchmarking detail can quietly zero the hit rate. `vllm bench serve --dataset-name custom` reads JSONL rows with a text `prompt` ([datasets.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/benchmarks/datasets/datasets.py)), and the server re-tokenizes the text. *(Inference:)* If tokenizing the joined text does not reproduce the chunk ID lists, C3 misses every time. Either verify round-trip tokenization per request as part of E5, or write a small async client that posts token-ID prompts to `/v1/completions` and timestamps the first streamed token. Whether `bench serve` can send pre-tokenized prompts was not verified.

## Fixing the evaluation before the first sweep

### E0: three exact checks, an identity case and a measured noise floor

The decided structure is the right one. Run E0 in fp32 with TF32 off as a pass/fail gate, and run bf16 separately to measure a noise floor rather than demand exactness. *(Inference, for concrete tolerances:)* Check (a), the round trip by +Δ then −Δ, should agree to a few fp32 ulps of |k|. Check (b), first-layer keys shifted versus computed directly at the target position, needs the position-aware tolerance from the RoPE section (atol ≈ c·*p*·6e-8·|k|) or an fp64 reference for both sides. Check (c), 100% recompute against full-prefill logits on the same SDPA backend, should agree to roughly 1e-5 relative. The added identity-case test closes the gap between unit tests and the full path in two ways. A rotation by Δ = 0 has cos = 1 and sin = 0, so it must return the stored bf16 keys **bit-for-bit**. And a chunk prefilled with exactly the context it later follows (same system prefix, same positions) must give zero-recompute logits equal to full prefill within the check (c) tolerance. If those pass and method 1 still diverges, the divergence is the real cross-chunk error that E1 measures. For bf16, compute the distance between HF's own bf16 and fp32 full prefill (the noise floor), then report reuse error as a multiple of that floor, using KL and top-1 agreement rather than exact values. HF's bf16 keys carry about 1e-2 relative noise, because cos and sin are cast to bf16 before the multiply. So a correct fp32-rotated cache can sit closer to the true value than HF's reference does.

### Quality: official scorer, per-category headline, paired intervals

Use LoCoMo's `evaluation.py` verbatim. Its normalization removes commas and punctuation, drops "a/an/the/**and**", and **Porter-stems** tokens before token F1. Category 1 (multi-hop) scores the mean over comma-split gold sub-answers of the best-matching prediction. Category 3 keeps only the first `;`-separated gold answer. Category 5 (adversarial) is a substring check for "no information available" or "not mentioned" ([evaluation.py](https://github.com/snap-research/locomo/blob/main/task_eval/evaluation.py)). The decision to exclude category 5 from the headline is well founded, because a verbose model can pass that substring check by accident. Report category 5 accuracy separately, using the repo's (a)/(b) multiple-choice format ([gpt_utils.py](https://github.com/snap-research/locomo/blob/main/task_eval/gpt_utils.py)). Count the questions in categories 1-4 yourself rather than relying on the commonly cited 1,540. The code comments label 1 as multi-hop and 2 as temporal. Confirm the names of 3 and 4 from the LoCoMo paper before labelling plots.

Make per-category F1 a headline figure, not an ablation. AgentKVShift reports that all reuse methods, including its own, "leave a wider gap… on cross-chunk reasoning capabilities such as Causal Inference and State Updating" ([AgentKVShift](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21604)). Cestola et al. find recompute methods 7-18% below full prefill and base models scoring F1 = 0 on 42-58% of multi-hop queries, which "masks true technique effectiveness" ([2603.20218](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.20218)). No paper found reports LoCoMo per-category reuse numbers, so this is open ground. Following that study, also report the share of questions where full prefill already scores 0, and the gap restricted to questions full prefill answers.

*(Inference, for statistics:)* Greedy F1 runs are deterministic up to kernel noise, so "median of three" adds little there. Spend repetitions on latency instead. For method differences, use a **paired bootstrap over questions**. Because questions within one conversation are correlated and there are only 10 conversations, also run a conversation-level (cluster) bootstrap and report the wider interval. Differences of 0.02 F1 may not survive it. Fix decoding for every method: the repo's QA prompt, `max_new_tokens` ≈ 32, stop at the first newline, and the same answer post-processing for all. Report mean answer length per method as a sanity check, because Mistral-Instruct's tendency to write full sentences hurts F1 precision. AgentKVShift's generation settings and category-5 handling are unstated in the parts read, which limits how directly the 0.509/0.491 numbers compare.

For tuning *k* and *r* cheaply, add a fidelity metric *(inference)*. Teacher-force the full-prefill greedy answer after each method's assembled cache, then record the mean per-token KL(p_full ‖ p_method), the answer log-likelihood, and first-token top-1 agreement. That costs one ~20-token forward pass per question, has no F1 noise, and gives smooth curves against budget. KL on the first generated token alone needs only prefill logits. Use it to pick operating points, then confirm those points with F1.

### Fair baselines, including the one prefix caching wins

Adding a full-conversation-in-context baseline is the most important evaluation change. It also reframes the serving comparison *(inference)*. A LoCoMo conversation averages about 16K tokens (proposal §4.1, citing [LoCoMo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.17753)), within Mistral's 32,768-token window ([config.json](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Mistral-7B-Instruct-v0.3/blob/main/config.json)). Verify that the longest conversation fits. This baseline is the quality reference for whether retrieval helps at all. It is also the configuration where **prefix caching wins**, because an append-only conversation is always a prefix of its own future, so APC (vLLM's automatic prefix caching) reuses it across questions. Its cost sits in decode and memory instead: about 2 GB of KV per request at 128 KiB/token, and attention is about 24% of prefill FLOPs at 16K (*est.*, from the arithmetic below). The honest framing is that non-prefix reuse targets *retrieved* memory, where order varies, and the report should show both regimes.

For E3 and E4, run four vLLM configurations: (a) full prefill, no connector, `--no-enable-prefix-caching`; (b) APC only; (c) connector with APC off, so every reuse pays load and re-rotation honestly; (d) connector plus APC, reported as the deployable setting. Give prefix caching its best case as well *(inference)*. A sensible operator could sort retrieved memories chronologically, which raises APC hits but changes the prompt, so measure its F1 too. LMCache CPU offload (`LMCacheConnectorV1`, `LMCACHE_CHUNK_SIZE=256`) or vLLM's native `offloading_connector` gives an exact-order offload baseline ([lmcache example](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/examples/disaggregated/lmcache/cpu_offload_lmcache.py)). Reordered chunks reportedly get near-zero hits on that path in 0.27.1 (0.709 s against a 0.728 s cold prefill in [#5101](https://github.com/LMCache/LMCache/issues/5101)).

Prefix-cache contamination is real but runs one way. When a connector load completes, vLLM hashes those blocks under the request's exact token hashes and registers them in its prefix cache: immediately for sync loads, after transfer for async ones ([kv_cache_manager.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/v1/core/kv_cache_manager.py), [scheduler.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/v1/core/sched/scheduler.py)). With APC on, a later request with the same chunks in the same order then gets a *local* hit on approximate KV, which shifts what configuration (d) measures. Isolate it with APC off, or a per-request `cache_salt`, which is mixed into the first block's hash ([kv_cache_utils.py](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/v0.27.1/vllm/v1/core/kv_cache_utils.py)). Restart the server between configurations. Never run an exactness check on a server that has served approximate KV. Read `num_external_cached_tokens` from the scheduler stats to separate "hit" from "fast".

Serving claims need open-loop arrivals: Poisson at several request rates, TTFT p50/p99 against achieved throughput, plus inter-token latency. Sarathi-Serve shows that prefill and decode interfere, and its chunked-prefill scheduling gives 2.6× more serving capacity for Mistral-7B on one A100 than vLLM did at the time ([Sarathi-Serve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02310)). *(Inference:)* Removing prefill should therefore also lower **other users'** decode-latency tail, so report ITL p99 in E4. Run one short-output and one long-output setting to show how output length dilutes the gain. Report the cold cost of prefilling and storing a chunk separately from the warm hit cost, and plot gain against **measured** hit rate, not just the Zipf exponent. Warm up, use CUDA events, take the median and IQR of 20 or more repetitions for microbenchmarks, and record GPU SKU, driver and clocks with every result.

## Predict, measure, explain: the interview story

### The numbers to know cold

Inference-infrastructure prep material keeps returning to the same questions: why prefill is compute-bound and decode memory-bound, how big the KV cache is and how to manage it, how PagedAttention and prefix caching work, and how to design a serving system ([techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233475331/ai-inference-infrastructure-interview-prep-2026-vllm-triton/)). Postings name the same skills. Inferact, the vLLM company, prefers "deep understanding of KV-cache memory management, prefix caching, and hybrid model serving" ([search summary](https://zerogtalent.com/ai-jobs/inferact/member-of-technical-staff-inference-9470565b-c62d-4de9-8b87-26d525ecec49)), and Lightbits has a role dedicated to KV-cache optimization in vLLM and SGLang ([Lightbits](https://www.lightbitslabs.com/position/senior-inference-systems-engineer-kv-cache-optimization/)). The project answers those questions with first-hand data. It says nothing about speculative decoding, quantization or tensor parallelism, which need separate preparation.

The arithmetic below uses Mistral's architecture (dim 4096, FFN 14336, 32 layers, 8 KV heads; [Mistral 7B](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06825)), kipply's per-token formulas ([kipply](https://kipp.ly/transformer-inference-arithmetic/)), and NVIDIA's spec sheets (A100: 312 TFLOPS dense bf16, about 2.0 TB/s HBM; H100 SXM: about 989 TFLOPS dense, 3.35 TB/s; [A100](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/a100/), [H100](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/data-center/h100/)). Every derived number is an estimate. Being able to rebuild this table on a whiteboard is the interview skill.

| Quantity | Value (*est.*) | How |
|---|---|---|
| Non-embedding parameters | 6.98B | 32 × (41.9M attention + 176.2M SwiGLU MLP) |
| Prefill compute | ≈14.0 GFLOP/token | 2 × parameters |
| KV size | **128 KiB/token** | 32 layers × 8 heads × 128 dims × 2 (K, V) × 2 bytes. A 32-KV-head model would be 512 KiB, so GQA is what makes loading cheap |
| Recompute avoided per byte loaded | ≈106,000 FLOP/byte | 14.0e9 / 131,072 |
| KV capacity on 80 GB | ≈440K tokens | 72 GB at 0.9 utilization, minus 14.5 GB of weights |
| Roofline ridge point | A100 ≈153, H100 ≈295 FLOP/byte | peak FLOPs ÷ HBM bandwidth. A pass over fewer tokens than this is memory-bound |
| Floor for any forward pass | A100 ≈6.8 ms, H100 ≈4.2 ms | read 14 GB of weights once |
| Full prefill, 2K tokens, 50% MFU | A100 ≈186 ms, H100 ≈59 ms | linear plus attention FLOPs ÷ achieved FLOP/s |
| Attention share of prefill FLOPs | 4% at 2K, 24% at 16K | 2·32·4096·N² against 14e9·N |
| Load vs recompute per token | 9-21× cheaper over PCIe | 75-112 µs recompute (A100) vs 5.2 µs at 25 GB/s |
| Break-even link bandwidth | A100 1.2-1.8 GB/s, H100 3.7-5.6 GB/s | achieved FLOP/s ÷ 106,496. It does not depend on prompt length |
| k=6 chunks (~1,800 tokens) | 9.4 ms from pinned host, 0.24 ms from HBM, ≈135-200 ms to recompute | 230 MB at 25 GB/s or about 2 TB/s |

Two inputs need measuring rather than assuming. Realistic H2D on an A100 PCIe Gen4 host is about 25 GB/s, but misconfigured systems measure far lower (one report saw 8.4 GB/s pinned) ([NVIDIA forums](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/a100-simplemulticopy/303902), [slow-H2D thread](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/pcie-bandwidth-issue-h2d-very-slow-gen1-but-d2h-reaches-gen4/345210)). Benchmark it on PACE. And PACE ICE lists A100s, while H100/H200 nodes appear under the separate AI Makerspace ([CoC proposal](https://support.cc.gatech.edu/sites/default/files/tech_fee_props/CoC%20-%20GPU%20Computing%20Expansion%20fore%20PACE-ICE.pdf), [AI Makerspace](https://www.coe.gatech.edu/academics/ai-for-engineering/ai-makerspace)). Confirm which GPU the course allocation actually provides before quoting H100 numbers. The LMCache paper reports a crossover around 32 Gbps (4 GB/s), above which loading beats prefill at all lengths ([LMCache](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.09665), snippet). That sits between the A100 and H100 break-evens above, but its GPU was not verified, so don't cite it as agreement yet.

*(Inference:)* The core interview insight follows from the table. With reuse, the idealized TTFT at *k* = 6 on an A100 is roughly 10-20 ms against about 190 ms for full prefill, an upper bound of about 10-15×. It cannot drop below one memory-bound pass over the question, about 7 ms. The measured speedup will be smaller because of the system prompt, the partial-block tail, scheduler steps, Python per-layer overhead, kernel launches and HTTP. Re-rotation reads and writes about 64 KiB per token of K, so it is never the bottleneck. Launch count can be. **Write the predicted number in the results log before each run, then explain the residual with a profiler trace.** That habit is what separates an inference engineer from someone who ran a benchmark.

## Conclusion

The research changes what the project is *about* more than how it is built. A year ago, "non-prefix reuse in vLLM" would have been the contribution. Now dozens of methods exist and the methods question is crowded, so the defensible core is measurement under honest accounting. That means one budget that charges selection layers, per-category F1 showing where cross-chunk reasoning breaks, conversation-clustered intervals, and a serving comparison that includes the regime where prefix caching wins: the full conversation in context. Those are also the habits inference teams hire for, so the evaluation fixes and the interview story are the same work.

The most useful mindset shift for a newcomer is to treat every number as a prediction to falsify. The roofline says reuse should give roughly a 10-15× TTFT reduction at best and can never beat a ~7 ms question prefill on an A100. Whatever the measurement shows, the explanation of the gap, traced to block alignment, launch overhead, scheduler steps or the cost of uncorrected quality, is the result that will carry an interview. The headline speedup will not.
